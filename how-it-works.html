How the experiment works

One search procedure, five versions of the same market data. This page describes what changes between the arms, what does not, and the checks applied to every result.

The search

In each round (a generation) the model proposes 8 formulas built from 50 technical indicators such as momentum, volatility, volume and distance from the 52-week high. Each new formula is backtested: every week, two weeks or month (the formula chooses), it buys the 5 highest-ranked Dow stocks in equal weights, trading at the next day's open with costs of 0.10% per round trip. Results from the training years (2011–2018) and the validation years (2019 – September 2024) are fed back to the model, which then proposes new formulas or edits and combines earlier ones.

The transformations

Illustration with made-up prices, not market data. The time axis runs left to right in every panel.

  • Reverse: bar on trading day t = original bar of day N-1-t; date labels keep the original increasing calendar; within each bar open and close swap (O' = C, C' = O); high, low, volume and Dow membership stay with the bar; DIA and SPY closes reversed the same way.
  • Invert: P' = P0^2 / P per series, P0 = first valid adjusted close in the arm's timeline; O' = P0^2/O, C' = P0^2/C, H' = P0^2/L, L' = P0^2/H; volume and membership unchanged; DIA and SPY inverted with their own P0.
  • Reverse + invert: reverse, then invert (P0 = first valid close of the reversed series).
  • EW_DOW recomputed in every arm from that arm's transformed closes and membership (daily rebalanced, no costs).
  • Prices are dividend (total-return) adjusted before any transformation.
  • Arm 5 (real control): no transformation; reads the same snapshot as Arms 2-4.

Each arm is judged against its own benchmarks, transformed the same way: the Dow ETF (DIA), the S&P 500 ETF (SPY) and an equal-weighted portfolio of the Dow members (EW_DOW) recomputed from the arm's own prices.

What is held identical

  • Everything in config.yaml as it stands when this plan is committed: indicators (50, same names and descriptions), formula language and limits, backtest (top 5, long only, signal at close, trade at next open, 5 + 5 bps, rebalance chosen per formula: W / 2W / M), splits and walk-forward folds, fitness weights and penalties, duplicate rules, direction check (threshold 0.2), family rules, DSR, loop settings (8 proposals per generation, mix 60 / 25 / 15 new / mutate / combine, context sizes), prompts, model routing (new ideas → qwen3:30b-a3b, mutations and combinations → qwen3:14b), temperatures and output caps.
  • Arms 2–5 differ from each other only in the data transform. Each has its own database, and its arm name is part of its config hash (arm 1's hash 9a67f1776d65 is unchanged).
  • Price data for arms 2–5: one frozen copy of the research rows of the price cache, made once before the first of these arms runs (data/arms/snapshot/, only dates before 2024-10-01); its SHA-256 is recorded in each arm's run records and checked on every load. Arms 2–5 read only that copy.
  • Ollama sampling seed: llm.ollama_seed = 20261030 in arms 2–5, sent with every request. Arms 2–5 send the same first prompt (no formulas tested yet); later prompts differ because results differ.
  • Stop rule per arm: the loop stops at the end of the generation in which the arm reaches 300 evaluated formulas, or after 150 generations, whichever comes first. Failed generations are kept and counted. An arm that stops at 150 generations with fewer than 300 formulas is analysed with what it has, and every output says so.
  • Seed of the random groups in the transfer test (section 8, item 16): experiment.transfer_seed = 20261111 plus the arm number, i.e. 20261113 (arm 2), 20261114 (arm 3), 20261115 (arm 4). Recorded in config.yaml before any of these arms ran; the seeds are printed in methods.md.

5.1 Known differences between arm 1 and arms 2–5 (recorded, not corrected)

Arm 5 exists so that arms 2–4 can also be compared with a real-data arm that has none of these differences.

  • Arm 1's first 204 evaluated formulas were produced while settings were still changing (e.g. model routing, prompt wording on z-scores and on mutations, proposal mix, family penalty, direction threshold, salvage fixes); its remaining formulas use the final settings. Arms 2–5 use the final settings from generation 1.
  • Arm 1 read the live price cache, refreshed at different times (a refresh can revise dividend-adjusted price levels); arms 2–5 read the frozen snapshot.
  • Arm 1 requests were sent without a sampling seed.
  • Arm 1's run had interruptions and restarts (e.g. an Ollama CUDA error) under the earlier settings; these are kept in its record.

Isolation between the arms

  • One SQLite database per arm. No arm reads another arm's database while running.
  • The LLM is never told which arm it is in: prompts are built by the same code with the same indicator names and descriptions; the arm name appears only in the database file name and run records, which are never sent to the model.
  • No result from one arm is ever placed in another arm's prompts.
  • Arm-to-arm comparison happens only in the compare-arms command, after the arms have run, and its output is never shown to the model.
  • The research-data versions of the transfer and decline tests (section 8, items 16–17) are computed inside compare-arms only, in memory. Nothing is written to any arm's database, so no formula's result on another arm's data can reach a prompt.
  • The holdout versions are computed only by evaluate-holdout, in the single holdout evaluation, after all arms have stopped. compare-arms never loads holdout prices; it reports the numbers saved by that evaluation.

Stop rule

Each arm stops at the end of the generation in which it reaches 300 tested formulas, or after 150 generations, whichever comes first. Failed generations are kept and counted. An arm that stops at the generation limit with fewer than 300 formulas is analysed with what it has, and every output says so. Proposals that rank stocks almost exactly like an earlier formula are rejected as duplicates and do not count.

The holdout plan and its amendments

The last two years of data (1 October 2024 – 15 October 2026) are locked away and are never transformed or seen by any arm's search. Safeguards

  • Counting every attempt. Every formula the model proposes is recorded, including the bad ones, and the deflated Sharpe ratio adjusts the best result for how many formulas were tried.
  • Consistency, not one lucky stretch. The validation period is split into yearly pieces, and a formula is rewarded for working in each of them.
  • Penalties for the usual tricks. Fitness is reduced for heavy trading, needless complexity, deep drawdowns, betting on company size, results much better on validation than on training, and being one more copy of an already crowded idea.
  • Duplicate rejection. A proposal that ranks stocks almost exactly like an earlier formula is rejected before it is backtested, so rewording an old idea does not count as a new discovery.
  • No peeking at the future. Every indicator is tested automatically for use of future data, and trades happen at the next day's open after the signal.
What is measured (plan section 8)

Analysis set per arm: the first 300 evaluated formulas (by formula id, i.e. evaluation order) under that arm's config hash. Within that set, the deflated Sharpe ratio is recomputed with exactly N = 300 trials and the set's own cross-trial Sharpe variance, and family crowding and fitness (including the family penalty) are recomputed among the set (same rules as the loop). Formulas an arm evaluated beyond its first 300 are not analysed.

Per arm:

1. Validation Sharpe ratio: count, mean, standard deviation, minimum, quartiles, maximum; histogram and empirical distribution. 2. Deflated Sharpe ratio: same statistics; number ≥ 0.95. 3. Train Sharpe ratio: same statistics. 4. Train–validation gap (validation Sharpe − train Sharpe) per formula: same statistics; and the per-generation top-10 averages recorded by the loop (validation, train, gap, luck line) up to the generation that reached 300. 5. Top 10 by recomputed fitness: averages of validation Sharpe, train Sharpe, gap, deflated Sharpe, validation CAGR, validation CAGR minus own EW_DOW CAGR, size exposure; their expressions. 6. Share of formulas with validation (and train) CAGR above the arm's own EW_DOW. 7. Families among the top 50: number, size of the largest, size distribution, and the top 5 families' representative expressions and indicator sets. 8. Indicators: for each of the 50 indicators, the share of the top-50 formulas containing it, and the median validation Sharpe of all formulas containing it. 9. Proposal outcomes: proposed, evaluated, duplicates, invalid, failed generations, generations needed to reach 300; duplicate rate by model. 10. Direction check: number of formulas checked and number contradicting their own rationale (threshold 0.2). 11. The arm's own benchmark CAGRs (DIA, SPY, EW_DOW) in train and validation.

Between arms (all ten pairs):

12. Differences in the per-arm statistics of items 1–6, listed for every arm against arm 1 and against arm 5. 13. Mann–Whitney U test (two-sided) and Kolmogorov–Smirnov statistic for the validation Sharpe, deflated Sharpe and gap distributions; p-values reported as computed, no multiple-comparison correction applied. 14. Indicators: the arm × indicator tables of item 8 side by side. 15. Families: Jaccard overlap of the indicator sets of each pair of arms' top 5 family representatives; number of identical canonical expressions present in both arms.

Formulas from arms 2–4 on the real data. Items 16 and 17 are each reported in two versions, for the same seven groups: (a) research data — every real research year was seen by some arm in transformed form (see "Real years covered" below) — and (b) holdout data, 2024-10-01 – 2026-10-15, which no arm has seen. Version (b) is defined after item 17.

16. Transfer test. Version (a): - Groups (7 groups, 70 formulas): for each of arms 2, 3 and 4, (a) its top 10 by fitness recomputed within its analysis set (the same ten as item 5) and (b) a random 10 from the rest of its analysis set; plus the top 10 of arm 5 as the real-data reference. - Random 10: the analysis set's formula ids without the top 10, sorted ascending; random.Random(seed).sample(ids, 10) in Python, with the seed of section 5 (the same method as the random group of docs/HOLDOUT_PLAN.md). The draw is made once per arm and cannot be re-drawn without changing the recorded seed. If fewer than 10 formulas remain, all of them are taken. - Data: the real research data, i.e. arm 5's data — the untransformed research rows of the frozen snapshot (SHA-256 checked, dates before 2024-10-01 only). The holdout is never loaded; the code refuses a panel that reaches it. - Evaluation: each formula keeps its expression and its own rebalance frequency; backtest rules, costs, train and validation windows and benchmarks are those of arm 5. A formula that cannot be evaluated on the real data is listed with its error and left out of its group's averages; the count evaluated is reported per group. - Reported per group: mean and median validation Sharpe ratio on the real data; number of formulas with validation Sharpe above 0; mean train Sharpe; mean deflated Sharpe ratio; mean validation CAGR minus real EW_DOW CAGR and the same for train (percentage points per year); and, for reference, the group's means in its own arm (validation Sharpe, deflated Sharpe, validation CAGR minus its own EW_DOW). Every formula's own numbers are listed as well. - Deflated Sharpe on the real data: computed from the formula's daily validation returns on the real data (Sharpe, skewness, kurtosis, number of days) with the number of trials and the cross-trial Sharpe variance of arm 5's analysis set (N = 300), for every group. - Check: arm 5's top 10 are re-evaluated the same way; the largest absolute difference from their stored validation Sharpe is reported. - Real years covered (from section 3.1): the test's validation window is real 2019-01-01 – 2024-09-30 and its train window is real 2011 – 2018. Arms 2 and 4 searched with real 2015-10-01 – 2023-09-28 as their train window, real 2023-09-29 – 2024-09-30 as their warm-up and real 2010-01-04 – 2015-09-30 as their validation window, each running backward. Arm 3 searched on the same real dates as the test, with inverted prices. Arm 5's top 10 were selected by fitness on the same data the test uses; the groups of arms 2–4 were selected on transformed data.

17. Decline test, for the same seven groups and the same backtests as item 16. Version (a): - Decline periods: taken from the real EW_DOW level (arm 5's) in the validation window, re-based to 1 at the close of the last trading day before the window. A decline is one unbroken spell below the running peak. Its days run from the first trading day after the peak through the trough, inclusive, where the trough is the lowest close before the peak is regained or the window ends. The 5 declines with the most negative peak-to-trough return are used (ties go to the earlier one). They cannot overlap. "Other days" are all remaining days of the validation window. - Reported per formula and averaged per group: mean daily net return on decline days and on other days, and the difference (basis points per day); mean daily return minus EW_DOW's daily return on decline days and on other days, and the difference (basis points per day); and for each of the 5 declines, the compounded return over its days, EW_DOW's compounded return over the same days, and the difference. - Also reported: the 5 declines (peak date, first day, trough date, trading days, EW_DOW return, recovery date if any), the number of decline days and other days, and EW_DOW's own mean daily return on each.

Version (b) of items 16 and 17: holdout data.

  • When and where: once, inside the single holdout evaluation (docs/HOLDOUT_PLAN.md, amendment 3), after all five arms have finished and only on the author's explicit request. Not computed by compare-arms.
  • Groups: the same seven groups and the same 70 formulas as version (a) — the same top 10s and the same seeded random 10s. Nothing is re-drawn.
  • Data: the real, untransformed data, loaded as the holdout plan prescribes (price cache refreshed with fetch-data immediately before). Each formula is backtested continuously from 2010 with its expression and its own rebalance frequency; only returns dated 2024-10-01 – 2026-10-15 are scored. Rules, costs and benchmarks as in arm 1 and arm 5.
  • Item 16 on the holdout, per group: mean and median holdout Sharpe ratio; number of formulas with holdout Sharpe above 0; mean deflated Sharpe ratio; mean holdout CAGR minus real EW_DOW's holdout CAGR (percentage points per year); next to the group's version (a) numbers and its own-arm numbers. Every formula's own numbers are listed as well.
  • Deflated Sharpe on the holdout: computed from the formula's daily holdout returns (Sharpe, skewness, kurtosis, number of days) with the same number of trials and cross-trial Sharpe variance as version (a) (arm 5's analysis set).
  • Item 17 on the holdout: the same definition of a decline applied to the real EW_DOW level inside the holdout period, re-based to 1 at the close of the last trading day before 2024-10-01; the 5 declines with the most negative peak-to-trough return; "other days" are the remaining days of the holdout period. The same measures as version (a), in the same units.
  • Real years covered: no arm's warm-up, train or validation window contains any day of the holdout period, in any arm; this also holds for arm 5's top 10.
  • The holdout plan's selection rule, comparison groups and success criterion are those of arm 1 alone and are not changed by these groups.
