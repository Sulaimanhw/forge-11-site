The final test: two years of market data that no search in any arm has seen and that were never used for any decision.

What it will test

  1. The original search (arm 1). Five formulas chosen by a rule fixed in advance: the top 5 by fitness, leaving out formulas that contradict their stated rationale and formulas whose daily ranking correlates above 0.8 with one already chosen. Compared with the median formula, 5 formulas drawn at random (seed 20261010) and the top 5 under the original direction rule. The pre-registered success criterion: the combined five have a holdout Sharpe ratio above 0 and a yearly return above the equal-weighted Dow (EW_DOW).
  2. The transfer test on the holdout. The same 70 formulas as on the Results page — the top 10 and a random 10 from each transformed arm, plus the real control's top 10 — tested on the real holdout data. Nothing is re-drawn.
  3. The decline test on the holdout. The same formulas during the largest declines of the equal-weighted Dow in the holdout period.

# Forge 11 — Pre-registered Holdout Evaluation Plan

_Written 2026-10-10, before the long research run. This file is committed to
the repository before any holdout data is evaluated. `python -m forge_11
evaluate-holdout` refuses to run unless this file exists and is committed
unchanged, and it records this file's commit hash with every holdout result._

## Why a pre-registered plan

The research loop has seen train (2011–2018) and validation (2019 to
2024-09-30) results for every formula it proposed, generation after
generation. Even with walk-forward folds, a Deflated Sharpe Ratio and penalties
for overfitting, the search can fit the validation period itself. The holdout
period is the only data no formula, no LLM prompt and no design decision has
seen. Deciding *in advance* how it will be used — which formulas, which
comparison, what counts as success — prevents choosing those after looking at
the results.

## Holdout data

- Period: from **2024-10-01** to the last trading day in the data cache when
  the evaluation runs. The cache is refreshed once (`fetch-data`) immediately
  before the evaluation.
- Same rules as the research period: point-in-time Dow membership, next-day
  open execution, top-5 long-only, 5 + 5 bps costs and slippage, each
  formula's own rebalance frequency, dividend-adjusted prices.
- Each formula is backtested continuously from 2010 with its fixed
  expression; only returns dated in the holdout period are scored.

## When

- **Once**, after the research database contains **at least 300 evaluated
  formulas** under the current configuration.
- The result of that first look is the headline result, whatever it shows.
  Any later look (for example after a bug fix) is recorded with its look
  number and a written reason and is reported alongside, never instead of,
  the first.

## Which formulas (selection rule)

Applied mechanically by the code to the database at evaluation time:

1. Rank all evaluated formulas by **fitness** (current configuration).
2. Exclude every formula with a **direction mismatch** (its rationale states a
   direction the formula does not follow). Formulas evaluated before the
   direction check existed are eligible.
3. Walk down the ranking and select a formula unless its daily stock-ranking
   correlation (signal similarity over the research period) with an
   already-selected formula exceeds **0.8**.
4. Stop at **5** selected formulas: the **top 5**.

Comparison groups, evaluated the same way:

- **Median formula** — the evaluated formula at the median fitness rank (the
  lower of the two middle ranks if the count is even).
- **Random 5** — five formulas drawn uniformly, with random seed **20261010**,
  from all evaluated formulas not in the top 5.

These show how much the selected formulas beat ordinary ones; if the top 5 do
no better than the random 5 out of sample, the selection added nothing.

## What is reported

For every evaluated formula: holdout Sharpe, CAGR, maximum drawdown,
turnover, excess return versus EW_DOW, DIA and SPY, and size-adjusted alpha
with its Newey-West t-statistic, next to the same formula's validation
results. Plus the combined top-5 portfolio (equal-weight average of the five
strategies' daily returns) and the averages of each group.

## What counts as success

**Primary criterion.** The combined top-5 portfolio has, over the holdout
period:

- a Sharpe ratio **above 0**, and
- a CAGR **above EW_DOW's** (the equal-weight Dow, which the strategy could
  have held instead).

Both must hold. Anything else is reported as a failure of the primary
criterion.

**Secondary results** (reported, not pass/fail): how many of the five
individually meet both conditions; the top-5 average holdout Sharpe versus
the random-5 average and the median formula; the drop from validation to
holdout Sharpe for each group.

## Honest limits stated in advance

About two years of daily data cannot establish statistical significance for
strategies with Sharpe ratios near 1; t-statistics will be reported but are
not part of the success criterion. Thirty large-cap stocks offer limited
diversification, and the result is one period of one market. A success is
evidence the method found something that survived out of sample, not proof
of a durable edge; a failure is a legitimate, reportable finding.

## Amendment 1 — 2026-10-12

_Made before any holdout data was evaluated. Original plan: commit `bd41f19`._

**Change: direction-mismatch threshold set to 0.2.** Step 2 of the selection
rule excludes formulas whose rationale states a direction the formula does
not follow. A mismatch is now counted only when the formula's average daily
rank correlation with a stated indicator has the opposite sign **and** an
absolute value of at least **0.2**. Until now the code used 0.05 (the original
plan did not state a value).

**Reason.** Most formulas multiply two indicators (e.g.
`dollar_volume_63 * trend_r2_126`). A product of two z-scores has almost no
rank correlation with either component on its own, so the correlations behind
most flags were between −0.05 and −0.10 — statistical noise rather than a
contradiction. At 0.05, 44 of 110 checked formulas (40%) were flagged,
including four of the top ten for that reason alone. Clear contradictions
(e.g. a formula said to buy low RSI that has +0.71 correlation with RSI)
remain flagged at 0.2.

**What had been seen.** The decision was made after viewing a *preview of the
selection rule* on research data (which formulas would be chosen, by ID, with
their train/validation metrics). No holdout returns, prices or metrics were
computed or viewed; the holdout table in the research database is empty.

**Safeguard against cherry-picking.** At holdout time the code also evaluates
and reports, as a separate group, the **top 5 selected with the original 0.05
threshold** (same rule otherwise). Both groups are reported side by side; the
primary criterion is applied to the 0.2-threshold top 5 as amended, and the
0.05 group's result is shown next to it.

**Other changes since the plan (for transparency).** On the same date, for
formulas proposed from now on: the LLM prompt explains that indicators are
cross-sectional z-scores; 60% of proposals are brand-new ideas; mutations and
combinations are proposed by a smaller model (`qwen3:14b`); and fitness gains
a penalty for formulas in a crowded family (many top formulas with signal
correlation above 0.8). Fitness — and therefore the ranking used in step 1 —
is computed with the configuration in force at holdout time, as the original
plan states.

## Amendment 2 — 2026-10-16

_Made before any holdout data was evaluated (the holdout table is still empty)
and before any paper-trading code was written or run._

**Holdout period fixed: 2024-10-01 to 2026-10-15 (inclusive).** The original
plan let the holdout run to "the last trading day in the cache when the
evaluation runs". It now ends on a fixed date, so that it cannot overlap with
paper trading. Holdout metrics use only returns dated 2024-10-01 through
2026-10-15, whenever the evaluation is run.

**Paper trading is a separate forward test starting 2026-10-16.** Paper
portfolios are live forward results: created on or after 2026-10-16, they
trade only on prices that did not exist when they were created. They use the
same rules as the backtest (top 5, equal weight, the formula's own rebalance
frequency, signal at the close and trade at the next open, 5 + 5 bps costs)
and are measured against DIA, SPY and EW_DOW. Paper results are never shown to
the language model and play no part in holdout selection. Each portfolio
records the commit of this amendment.

**First paper portfolios: the top 3 by fitness on 2026-10-16.** Chosen from
the **204** formulas evaluated at that date, **by fitness alone** (no other
criterion, no look at holdout or paper results):

| Rank | Formula | Fitness | Rebalance |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | #273 `dollar_volume_63 * trend_r2_126 + (dist_52w_high + intraday_ret_21) * vol_ratio_20_120 + (dist_52w_high + overnight_ret_21) * pv_corr_20` | +0.4962 | monthly |
| 2 | #133 `dollar_volume_63 * trend_r2_126 + overnight_ret_21 * vol_ratio_20_120 + pv_corr_20 * (1 - beta_252)` | +0.4809 | monthly |
| 3 | #51 `dollar_volume_63 * (trend_r2_126 + ema_gap_12_26)` | +0.4546 | monthly |

(Rank 4, #246, had fitness +0.4545 — the cut between ranks 3 and 4 is a
difference of 0.0001.) They are created after the 2026-10-16 close, so their
first trade is at the next open (2026-10-19), using the 2026-10-16 close as
the signal.

**Holdout selection will also be paper-traded.** When the holdout is
evaluated, the formulas selected by the rule above (the top 5, and for
comparison the top 5 under the original 0.05 direction rule) also get paper
portfolios starting the next trading day, so the same formulas have both a
holdout test and a live forward test.

## Amendment 3 — 2026-11-12

_Made before any holdout data was evaluated (the holdout table is still empty:
0 looks) and before any arm of the parallel worlds experiment other than arm 1
was run. At this date arm 1 (the research run this plan was written for) has
204 evaluated formulas; the price snapshot for arms 2–5 has not been made._

**Change 1: the one holdout look is made after all five arms have finished.**
The parallel worlds experiment (`docs/PARALLEL_WORLDS_PLAN.md`) runs the same
search in five arms: arm 1 is this research run; arms 2–4 search transformed
copies of the research data; arm 5 repeats the search on the untransformed
research data. The holdout is now evaluated only when:

- arm 1 has at least 300 evaluated formulas (unchanged), **and**
- each of arms 5, 2, 3 and 4 has reached its stop rule (300 evaluated formulas
  or 150 generations, whichever comes first), **and**
- the author explicitly asks for it (unchanged).

The holdout is still evaluated **exactly once**. The rules for any later look
(look number and written reason, reported alongside and never instead of the
first) are unchanged. The holdout period is unchanged: 2024-10-01 to
2026-10-15.

**Change 2: the same look also evaluates the seven transfer-test groups.**
In addition to the groups already in this plan, the single look evaluates the
seven groups of the parallel worlds plan (section 8, item 16), 70 formulas:

- for each of arms 2, 3 and 4: its top 10 by fitness within its first 300
  evaluated formulas, and a random 10 from the rest of those 300 (seeds
  20261113, 20261114 and 20261115, recorded in `config.yaml` on 2026-11-11);
- the top 10 of arm 5, as the real-data reference.

These groups are fixed mechanically by each arm's database, the fitness rule
and the recorded seeds. They are evaluated on the **real, untransformed**
holdout data under the rules of the "Holdout data" section above (each formula
keeps its expression and its own rebalance frequency). The holdout is never
transformed, and no arm's search ever loads it.

Reported for each of the seven groups and for each of their formulas: holdout
Sharpe ratio, holdout CAGR minus EW_DOW's, and deflated Sharpe ratio (item 16
of the parallel worlds plan), and returns during the 5 largest peak-to-trough
declines of the real EW_DOW inside the holdout period compared with the other
days of the holdout period (item 17), each next to the same numbers on the
research data. The exact definitions are in the parallel worlds plan, section
8.

**Unchanged.** The selection rule (top 5 of arm 1 by fitness, direction
threshold 0.2, signal correlation at most 0.8), the comparison groups (median
formula, random 5 with seed 20261010, top 5 under the original 0.05 rule) and
the primary success criterion, which applies only to arm 1's combined top 5.
The seven transfer-test groups are not part of the success criterion and are
reported as numbers only. They do not get paper portfolios; the paper
portfolios of amendment 2 (arm 1's holdout selection) start the trading day
after the look, as before, which is now later.

**Reason.** In the parallel worlds experiment every year of the research
period is seen by some arm in transformed form (the parallel worlds plan,
section 3.1, lists the real years each arm's train and validation windows
cover). The holdout period is the only period that no arm has seen. Because
this plan allows one look, the look has to wait until the formulas of all arms
exist.

**Consequences stated in advance.**

- Arm 1 stops at the end of the generation in which it reaches 300 evaluated
  formulas and is not run again before the look. The selection rule is applied
  to arm 1's database as it stands at the look.
- Results from arms 2–5 are never shown in arm 1's prompts and are stored in
  separate databases, so they cannot change arm 1's formulas, fitness or
  selection.
- The data cache is refreshed (`fetch-data`) immediately before the look, as
  before. Arms 2–5 searched on a frozen copy of the research data made
  earlier; a refresh can revise dividend-adjusted price levels, so their
  formulas' research-period numbers recomputed at the look can differ slightly
  from the stored ones. Both are reported.
- `evaluate-holdout` enforces this amendment mechanically: it refuses while
  any of arms 5, 2, 3 or 4 has not reached its stop rule.
- Nothing runs the holdout evaluation automatically. It runs only when the
  author asks for it.
